# Branched actin network in a 3D box, pushing a lid upward against a load.
#
# The box is 0.5 um wide in x and z and 2 um tall in y. It starts with 40 actin
# filaments whose minus ends are adsorbed to the floor at random positions and that point
# up toward a mobile lid. A constant downward force acts on the lid. The filaments'
# plus ends are barbed, so they elongate at rate v=a*min(exp(-k*F),2)-b, with
# a=0.122 um/s, k=0.00063 um/zJ, and b=0.0038 um/s, where F is the force pushing
# back on the end. Barbed ends get capped at rate 0.35/s, after which they stop
# growing. Filaments branch only within 0.05 um of the lid, at rate 0.425/s per node.
# Branches are new actin filaments, so they also grow, push, get capped, and branch.
#
# Units are um, s, and zJ, so forces are in zJ/um = fN.
#
# The viscosity is 250 zJ s/um^3, which is 1000 times the fluid viscosity of
# 0.25 zJ s/um^3 (0.25 mPa s). With the real value, short actin filaments tumble by
# several radians per millisecond, so the Implicit integrator divides most time steps into
# many substeps, and 20 s takes hours. The higher viscosity slows the filaments' thermal
# motions, but it doesn't change their equilibrium shapes, elongation, branching, or
# capping, or the lid's motion, which has its own mobility, so the results are similar and
# 20 s takes a few minutes.
#
# The mobility comes from the viscosity, but the rotational mobility is entered
# separately, because the value that the viscosity gives for 7 nm diameter segments makes
# segments roll by large angles in each time step. The twist dynamics don't matter here,
# and the rotational mobility doesn't change the equilibrium twist, so this uses a smaller
# value.
#
# smoldyn actinBox3D.txt
